The ritual

Scoop. Stir. Sip.

One jar. Two simple ingredients. Endless ways to enjoy. The whole practice takes ninety seconds — here it is, and eight ways to make it yours.

  1. Scoop

    One teaspoon of Honey with Fresh Ginger. Use a dry spoon, or the beechwood dipper — water is the one thing honey doesn’t want.

  2. Stir

    Into about 8 oz of warm water — not boiling. Stir until the spoon comes up clean and the ginger threads swirl up from the bottom.

  3. Sip

    Wait thirty seconds. Sweet first, then a slow, clean warmth at the back of the throat. Take the minute; it’s the point.

A note on warmth

Why warm, not boiling

Fresh ginger carries bright, almost citrusy top notes that fade quickly in boiling water. Somewhere around 130°F the honey dissolves instantly and the ginger stays lively. That one detail is most of the difference between a good cup and a great one.

Ritual questions

How much should I take?
One teaspoon is the classic serving. Some mornings want two. It’s food, not medicine — use what tastes right.
Warm or hot water?
Warm — around 120–140°F. Boiling water mutes the fresh ginger. Let the kettle stand a minute, or mix hot with a splash of cold.
Can I take it straight?
Absolutely. Off the spoon is how our mother often took it. Sweet first, then the ginger’s warmth.
When is the best time?
Whenever you’ll actually do it. Morning before the phone is our habit; the evening cup in warm water or rooibos is the other end of the same ritual.
Is it suitable for kids?
Not for infants under 12 months (no honey of any kind). Older children usually love a half-teaspoon in warm milk or on toast.
